\chapter{AEGISS selection (menu 25)}
\label{ch:menu25}
\menulabel{25}

\section{Purpose}

The AEGISS workflow (Tarocco et al., arXiv 2026) joins the two complementary
selection ideas: \emph{how correlated} an orbital is (the single-orbital
entropy of the AutoCAS family) and \emph{whether it belongs to the chemistry
of interest} (the atomic-orbital projection of the AVAS family).  The result
is a compact, chemically anchored active space: an entropy screen keeps every
orbital above a relative line $\tau_E = \tau\, S_{\max}$, and a projection
onto the chosen AO family keeps the entropy-screened orbitals above a weight
threshold.

\section{What you need}

The \code{orca\_2json} export of the CASSCF gbw (with $S$ and the orbital
labels), the same run's FCIDUMP (the exact entropies of the four-state-entropy
route), optionally the run's output for the energy cross-check, and the AO
label of the chemistry of interest (\code{C pz}, \code{Fe d}, \code{N p}).

\section{What you get}

The five-step trace --- every window orbital's occupation, exact entropy,
screen verdict, projection weight and final verdict --- the resulting
$(n_{\mathrm{e}}, n_{\mathrm{o}})$, and a report
(\file{<export>.aegiss.fbk.md}) with the citations.

\section{How to read it}

\begin{readbox}
\begin{itemize}
  \item the entropy screen keeps every orbital strictly above
    $\tau\, S_{\max}$ (a relative line, so it transfers between systems);
    the source's default $\tau = 0.1$ is its AutoCAS-style rule, and its
    benzene example used $0.2$;
  \item \textbf{the projection weight here is the projection norm}
    $\sum_\eta |[O]_{\eta p}|^2$ (the standard AVAS reading of the same
    overlap matrix).  The source writes it as a signed row sum; measured on
    the benzene fixture that sum cancels by symmetry for every nodal $\pi$
    orbital (only the nodeless $a_{2u}$ survives), so this tool uses the norm
    and states the deviation in every report.  On the same fixture the norm
    separates cleanly: $\sigma$ orbitals measure exactly $0.0000$, the
    $\pi$ manifold $0.05$--$3.09$;
  \item the AO label's shell index is optional: \code{C pz} matches every
    $p_z$ shell of every carbon (12 functions), \code{C 2pz} only the second
    shell (6).  Measured: the whole-family label keeps all six $\pi$ orbitals
    at the $0.5$ threshold --- the textbook $(6e, 6o)$ --- while the
    shell-resolved label narrows the space; that is the resolution knob the
    label offers;
  \item \textbf{scope, stated in the report}: the entropies are exact over
    the FCIDUMP window (the source estimates them from a DMRG over a larger
    window), and the target is the calculation's own AO subset (the
    non-minimal AVAS route of menu~\menu{14}, the only one open to the
    $f$ block).  Multi-group unions are run one group per pass; the selection
    is delivered as the window orbitals to include, and feeding it to a
    CASSCF needs the orbital-order machinery (menu~\menu{22}'s boundary
    note).
\end{itemize}
\end{readbox}

\section{Measured anchor}

On the benzene/cc-pVDZ $\pi$ platform (an AVAS-prepped CASSCF window; the
fixtures \file{benzene.json} and \file{benzene.fcidump}), the exact window FCI
energy reproduces the engine's printed CASSCF energy to eleven digits
($-230.793818898$), the source's own benzene CASSCF value $-230.793770$
agrees to $5\times10^{-5}$, and the whole-family label recovers the
$(6e, 6o)$ $\pi$ space --- the source's own headline result for this molecule.
