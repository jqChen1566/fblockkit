\chapter{TNASS subset selection (menu 26)}
\label{ch:menu26}
\menulabel{26}

\section{Purpose}

TNASS (Mingare, Heuzé and Coveney, arXiv 2026) selects the active space as
the spatial-orbital subset $A$ that \emph{maximizes the Rényi-2 entropy}
$S_2(A) = -\log \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_A^2)$ of the bipartition between $A$ and its
complement --- a genuinely multi-orbital entanglement measure that goes beyond
the single-orbital entropy of the AutoCAS family.  The selection algorithms
are pure combinatorics on top of that oracle: the exact combinatorial search
over $\binom{N}{n}$ subsets, the greedy method (start empty, repeatedly add
the orbital maximizing $S_2(A \cup \{i\})$), and the block greedy family
($k = 1$: the single-orbital ranking; $k = n$: plain greedy).

\section{What you need}

The FCIDUMP of a converged CASSCF run (the same dump the entropy and QICAS
routes read), optionally the run's output for the energy cross-check, the
target size (number of active spatial orbitals) and the method.

\section{What you get}

The one-orbital $S_2$ seed ranking, the step-by-step trace of the selection
with each subset's $S_2$, the resulting space and its electron count, and a
report (\file{<FCIDUMP>.tnass.fbk.md}) with the citations.

\section{How to read it}

\begin{readbox}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{the $S_2$ oracle is exact over the FCIDUMP window} --- the
    four-state-entropy route's determinant CI, grouped by the subset's
    occupation pattern.  The source builds the entanglement feature as a
    tensor network from a low-bond-dimension DMRG state over the full space
    (its useful bond dimensions are 4--6); there is no bond-dimension
    truncation here, but the window boundary is the restriction instead;
  \item the measured structural anchors: the one-orbital $S_2$ reproduces
    $-\log\sum_k w_k^2$ from the four-state weights to $10^{-12}$, and the
    bipartition symmetry $S_2(A) = S_2(\text{complement})$ holds for every
    subset on both fixtures;
  \item greedy cannot backtrack: on both fixtures its trace shows an $S_2$
    \emph{drop} on the last addition, and the brute-force optimum beats it
    (benzene $0.23280$ vs $0.23000$; N2 $0.13134$ vs $0.12663$) --- the
    source acknowledges this weakness and keeps the exact search for small
    windows (capped here at $\binom{N}{n} \le 20000$);
  \item the subset is a spatial-orbital set (both spins travel together),
    matching the source's selection domain; the source's best-$k$ variant
    (choose $k$ by the CASCI energy) is registered as a follow-up --- run the
    menu at several target sizes and compare;
  \item delivering the space to a CASSCF needs the orbital-order machinery
    (Chapter~\ref{ch:menu22}'s boundary note).
\end{itemize}
\end{readbox}
